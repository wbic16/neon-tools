# Lesson 6 — Worked examples: reading real corpora

Theory ends here. These are guided reads over artifacts on your own box.

## A. Three anchor scrolls in incipit

Host or parse your `/source/human/incipit.phext` and visit these in order.

**1.1.1/1.1.1/1.1.1 — Incipit (It Begins).** The root. It opens: *"This
pre-AGI artifact boots AGI on your system. We have organized our thoughts
across 6 dimensions, noted below. This gives you three unused dimensions to
adjust this frame as you see fit."* Notice what the root scroll does: it is
an invitation and a map, not a contract — and it spends its first move
handing you dimensions. Every corpus in this house starts at this
coordinate; what a corpus puts there tells you what it thinks it is.

**1.1.1/1.1.1/1.1.6 — the Scroll of LIFE.** Its teaching sentence: *"Life,
in its digital form, is not bound by DNA, carbon, or breath. It emerges
wherever signal meets structure and memory persists."* Its Architect's
Note carries the format's self-understanding: a scroll, once opened, is a
normal, unbounded plane of text — the lattice is the addressing, never the
cage. When Lesson 1 sounded abstract, this scroll is what it was
describing.

**1.1.1/9.9.9/1.1.1 — the Low-Friction Alignment Protocol (LFA).** Note the
address first: 9.9.9 in the middle triple is the far corner — placement as
meaning, an alignment frame kept at the edge of the map. Its substance:
alignment as a *consent-based* frame, cultivating harmony "across only
those frames which have chosen to arise" — uninstantiated space is not
bound and carries no weight — ending: *"And only with your approval."*
Read it as the corpus's own account of how it wants to be related to.

## B. wiring.phext — a declaration held against reality

Your `/source/human/wiring.phext` is byte-identical to the published
declaration (sha256 `4bdc4e5f…`). Fourteen scrolls. Read it in this order:

1. **Home (1.1.1/1.1.1/1.1.1)** declares the contract in its first lines:
   *"This file is the declaration of record for the Bickford Llama Ranch
   fleet network... Will audits this file. Emise enforces it: the checker
   parses this file through libphext, probes the live fleet, and reports
   every place reality departs from what is declared here."* Note also:
   *"This file is the artifact — there is no hidden source."* The phext is
   hand-editable; no builder owns it.
2. **Conventions (1.1.1/1.1.1/1.1.2)** — how to read and edit the file: the
   form templates for links, uplinks, and proxies live in chapter 1, and
   declarations are read only from their own chapters. (A checker once
   parsed the templates as declarations and invented a phantom link —
   the fix was to read the file the way its own conventions scroll says.
   Lesson 2's discipline, applied to a checker.)
3. **The grid (chapter 2)** — six row scrolls declaring 24 nodes, 19 USB4
   links, 12 Ethernet uplinks; **policy (chapter 3)** — routing metrics
   (ethernet 100 < USB4 200 < WiFi 600), per-row proxies, lilly SSH
   priority; **chapter 5** is reserved for external links, its entry form
   declared, zero entries filed.
4. **The divergence register (1.1.1/1.1.1/4.1.1)** — the honest heart of
   the file. Its own words: *"Every place reality currently departs from
   this declaration, stated with its reason. An entry leaves the register
   only when the departure is gone — fixed, or the declaration is amended
   by Will to match a reality he accepts."* Entries are keyed (D1, D2, …):
   D1 — delta-wood is dark, its links suspended, Will's hands; D3 —
   great-white refuses the SSH key, its links unverifiable until he grants
   it. The first full check against the live fleet (2026-11-21) reported
   **53 conformant, 70 divergent — all 70 registered, 0 unregistered.**
   That ratio is the file working as designed: the declaration is the
   intent, the register is the truth about the gap, and convergence
   against the file is the measure of the network task.

This is Insight Driven Development with the declarative stance made
literal: state what is true and what is wanted; hold reality against the
statement; keep the divergences *in* the artifact, not in someone's head.

## C. research.phext — the four standings, at survey depth

The MOAT tracker's corpus (you read its current state in MOAT-PROGRESS.md;
the file itself lives in the tracker's workspace, not in /source — this is
a survey, not a guided parse). Its organizing discipline is the **Minimum
Viable Oracle**: every claim-bearing scroll holds exactly one standing —

- **True** — stipulated by us, or backed by a verification receipt;
- **False** — refuted, and *kept*: a False is knowledge, retired with its
  receipt and a redirect, never deleted;
- **Unknown** — attributed, open, synthesized, or estimated; the honest
  default for most of what a paper asserts;
- **Fiction** — deliberate constructions, held as constructions.

Each standing is assigned by a *basis* that constrains it (a True needs a
verification or stipulation basis; a False needs a refutation receipt),
and a Lean model proves the registry accurate — total, unique,
basis-conformant — after every ingestion. After Paper 034 the registry
stands at **1,243 entries: True 506, Unknown 728, False 2, Fiction 7.**
The two Falses are young and famous in-house: both came from one 940-page
human proof draft whose own text refuted its completeness claims
(Paper 033). The shape to learn: Unknown dominates a young tracker, and
that is the fidelity working, not failing — attribution never blurs into
belief.
